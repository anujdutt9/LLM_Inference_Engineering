NovaAI — Business & Platform Investigation, March–August 2026

Source: data/nova_ai.db (operations database, 2026-03-01 → 2026-08-31; as-of 2026-09-01).
Method: four parallel sql-analyst investigations (revenue/plans/churn · usage/latency/quality · incidents/deployments · support/feedback), reconciled cross-area; every disputed number re-verified with a fifth SQL pass. usage_events is a 1-in-1000 sample; "est." figures are ×1000.

Summary

NovaAI's revenue looked flat-to-stable from March to August 2026 ($6.54M → $6.60M), but the surface hid two structural facts. First, a June 3 inference-engine deployment (v2.8.0) silently cut the chunked-prefill token budget and 2.6×'d time-to-first-token for nova-reasoning prompts over 16K tokens; because fleet-wide median latency was unaffected, it went undetected for 63 days. The resulting SLA breaches produced $72.2K of June credits (84.8% of all June credits) and six large Enterprise accounts churned in July — 98% of the $716.6K of MRR lost that month — pulling July revenue down 7.8% to $6.15M. Second, August's "recovery" to $6.60M is an illusion: it is driven almost entirely by one customer's batch job (Helix Genomics, $422K usage charge, ~7.8M true requests) that saturated US-East and caused 37 of 38 August 503 errors; ex-Helix, August revenue was ~$6.17M, still the depressed July level. Two further deployment regressions (the May 19–21 Europe rate-limiter and the July 14–16 US-West router — the half-year's only SEV1) made July the worst month for support (1,058 tickets, 151 critical, 55.3% negative sentiment) and availability (99.878%, the lowest). The good news: model quality never degraded (all quality ratings stable), the August 10 KV-cache v2.0.0 doubled cache hit rate with no latency cost, and the Business plan grew +25.2% — but with Enterprise MRR down ~$702K/month structurally and only 2–4 Enterprise signups per month, the lost revenue is unrecoverable until new logos arrive.

The 5 most material findings

1The June 3 inference-engine regression cost ~$702K/month of Enterprise MRR — and 76% of all incident downtime

What caused it. DEP-0021 (inference-engine v2.8.0, deployed Jun 3) halved the per-step chunked-prefill token budget to improve decode fairness. Only long prompts (>16K tokens) were affected, so fleet-wide p50 TTFT stayed flat and no canary fired. Undetected for 63 days: deployed Jun 3, declared Jun 22 (INC-2026-0099), fixed Aug 5 (DEP-0041, v2.9.1).

Numbers.

  • Avg TTFT for nova-reasoning prompts >16K tokens: 1,516 ms (before Jun 3) → 4,037 ms (Jun 3–Aug 5, 2.6×) → 1,876 ms (after Aug 5).
  • Monthly TTFT P99: ~1,900–1,950 ms (Mar–May) → 4,440 ms (Jun) → 3,554 ms (Jul) → 5,830 ms (Aug, includes the Helix batch).
  • INC-2026-0099 ran 1,059 h = 76% of all 1,387.8 h of Mar–Aug incident downtime.
  • The six churned accounts' own nova-reasoning avg TTFT: ~1,185 ms (Mar–May) → 2,505 ms (Jun); their request volume also fell in June (935 vs ~1,250 sampled/mo) — disengagement before churn.
  • $72,245 of June credits went to the six = 84.8% of all June credits ($85,183).
  • The six churned Jul 3–17: Meridian Health $165K, Quantix Capital $140K, Brightline Retail $120K, Arcadia Logistics $105K, Solace Insurance $92K, Vantage Biolabs $80K = $702,000 MRR = 98.0% of July churned MRR ($716,622).
  • July revenue $6,146,887 = −7.8% vs June $6,666,982; Enterprise base subscription $4.86M (Jun) → $4.39M (Jul) → $4.25M (Aug).
  • Support: nova-reasoning ratings 3.81 (May) → 3.37 (Jun) → 3.24 (Jul) → 3.83 (Aug); latency-category feedback 207 → 372 → 427 items at avg 2.84–2.86; churn-risk critical tickets ("Evaluating alternative providers" 4 Jun + 8 Jul; "Reducing usage until latency is fixed" 6 + 5); "SLA violations for 20 consecutive days" escalations in June.

2August's "recovery" to $6.60M is one customer's batch job — and that job degraded US-East for everyone else

What caused it. Helix Genomics (Enterprise, US-East, customer 817) started a nova-reasoning + document_analysis batch on Aug 17, running daily 13:00–15:00 UTC through Aug 28. The shared queue had no admission control, so the batch saturated the region (INC-2026-0144, "noisy-neighbor batch workload"). Fixed by DEP-0052 (Aug 31): a dedicated batch queue with admission control.

Numbers.

  • Helix August usage charge: $422,161 (vs $963–1,588/mo Mar–Jul); total August invoice ≈ $540K.
  • 7,817 sampled requests in August (~7.8M true) vs 2–5 sampled/mo Mar–Jul; ~101B true input tokens (101.4M sampled).
  • August revenue $6,596,469; ex-Helix ≈ $6.17M — the July depressed level. August usage revenue $887.0K vs $485.3K (Jul); ex-Helix $464,865.
  • Platform impact during batch hours: US-East queue depth 258–484, GPU utilization 95–97%; 37 of 38 August 503s (all US-East, exactly on batch days); US-East p99 TTFT 1,600 → 17,205 ms (10.8×) in the 13:00–16:00 windows; other US-East customers mildly affected (P95 761 → 859 ms).
  • August volume: 51.4M requests (+20%), input tokens 248.0B (+72%); document_analysis 14.0% → 27.3% of traffic.
  • Support: 13 embeddings (nova-small) latency complaints Aug 17–28, avg rating 2.15.

3The half-year's only SEV1 was a router deployment (Jul 14–16, US-West) — and it made July the worst support month

What caused it. DEP-0037 (router v5.2.0, deployed Jul 14 07:40) switched to a weighted-least-loaded policy that herded nova-reasoning traffic onto ~20% of GPU workers until they hit 99% utilization and KV-cache saturation. Rolled back to v5.1.4 (DEP-0038) Jul 16 19:50. INC-2026-0109: 59.6 h, US-West, nova-reasoning.

Numbers.

  • US-West nova-reasoning, incident window vs control: avg TTFT 697 → 2,520 ms (3.6×); 429/529 rate 0.6% → 10.6%; prefix-cache hit 0.287 → 0.13.
  • July availability 99.878% — the lowest of the six months (others 99.978–99.985%).
  • Support: 1,058 tickets in July (151 critical) vs ~700/mo (~30 critical) baseline; 51/57/72/49 latency+error+429 tickets per day Jul 14–17 vs a 6–17/day baseline; negative+very-negative sentiment 55.3% (Jul) vs 43.7–46.6% other months; very-negative tickets 189 (Jul) vs 29–81.
  • SLA credits issued to affected Enterprise customers on July invoices (July credits $154.3K across 95 invoices — the highest of the half-year).
-- Router incident: incident window vs control (US-West, nova-reasoning, Jul 7–23)
SELECT CASE WHEN timestamp>='2026-07-14' AND timestamp<'2026-07-16 20:00:00'
            THEN 'incident' ELSE 'control' END p,
       COUNT(*), ROUND(AVG(ttft_ms),0),
       ROUND(100.0*SUM(status_code IN (429,529))/COUNT(*),1),
       ROUND(AVG(cache_hit),3)
FROM usage_events
WHERE gpu_region='US-West' AND model='nova-reasoning'
  AND timestamp>='2026-07-07' AND timestamp<'2026-07-23'
GROUP BY 1;
-- control: 697 ms, 0.6%, 0.287 (n=2,054) | incident: 2,520 ms, 10.6%, 0.13 (n=339)

4Deployment regressions are the dominant failure mode: 78.3% of all downtime, and the release process has no latency regression gate

What caused it. No single cause — a pattern. 25 of 146 incidents (17%) have root_cause 'deployment regression' but account for 1,086.6 of 1,387.8 h (78.3%) of downtime. All 4 rolled-back deployments (of 52) map to declared incidents, and the three components with rollbacks — inference-engine (2/10, 20%), router (1/6), rate-limiter (1/10) — are exactly the latency-sensitive ones. The worst case (DEP-0021) was not even flagged rolled_back=1; it was fixed forward 63 days later.

Numbers.

  • Root-cause totals: deployment regression 25 incidents / 1,086.6 h; GPU saturation 34 / 36.6 h (chronic but short-lived); networking 24 / 23.3 h; routing imbalance 9 / 66.1 h; rate-limiter misconfig 11 / 50.7 h.
  • 52 deployments Mar–Aug (5/5/8/10/9/11 per month) — steady cadence, no release spike; 4 rollbacks (7.7%).
  • The four rollbacks: inference-engine v5.2.7 (May 7), rate-limiter v3.4.0 (May 19 → INC-2026-0066: Europe agents 429 rate 0.8% → 29.8%, May 19–21), inference-engine v2.9.5 (Jul 10), router v5.2.0 (Jul 14 → INC-2026-0109).
  • Detection gap on the worst incident: deployed Jun 3, declared Jun 22 (19 days), fixed Aug 5 (63 days).
-- Downtime by root cause
SELECT root_cause, COUNT(*) n,
       ROUND(SUM((julianday(end_time)-julianday(start_time))*24),1) h
FROM incidents GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY h DESC;
-- deployment regression: 25, 1,086.6 h | GPU saturation: 34, 36.6 h | ...

-- Rollback rate per component
SELECT component, COUNT(*) n, SUM(rolled_back) rb
FROM deployments GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 3 DESC;
-- inference-engine 10/2 | router 6/1 | rate-limiter 10/1 | all others 0

5Structural mix shift: Business is the only growth engine (+25.2%); Enterprise is shrinking and the lost MRR is not being replaced

What caused it. Steady Business-plan acquisition (21–25 signups/mo, low churn) against Enterprise attrition (the June churn plus normal churn) with only 2–4 Enterprise signups per month.

Numbers.

  • Business: 345 → 413 customers; revenue $1,205,881 (Mar) → $1,509,888 (Aug), +25.2%.
  • Enterprise: 234 → 233 customers; $5,189,655 (Mar) → $4,917,276 (Aug), −5.1%; base subscription $4.86M (Jun) → $4.25M (Aug) after the six-account churn.
  • Pro: $142.9K → $169.3K. Overall signups healthy: 75/88/84/81/87/70 per month; net customer count positive every month.
  • With Enterprise MRR down ~$702K/mo structurally and only 2–4 Enterprise signups/mo, the churned June MRR is unrecoverable without new logo growth.
  • Payment health stable: failed 13–24/mo, overdue 41–55/mo; July overdue rose to $361K (includes the six churned enterprises' prorated July invoices, $164K).
-- Revenue and customer count by plan and month
SELECT billing_month, plan, COUNT(*) cust, ROUND(SUM(total_amount),0) revenue
FROM invoices JOIN customers USING(customer_id)
GROUP BY billing_month, plan ORDER BY billing_month, plan;
-- Business: $1,205,881 (Mar) -> $1,509,888 (Aug) | Enterprise: $5,189,655 -> $4,917,276

What went well

  • Model quality never degraded. Quality-category feedback ratings were stable all six months (nova-reasoning 4.38–4.46, nova-vision 3.88–4.05, nova-small 3.57–3.73). Every degradation this half-year was latency or capacity, not model quality.
  • KV-cache v2.0.0 (DEP-0043, Aug 10) — the first kv-cache component deployment — doubled the prefix-cache hit rate (0.25 → 0.447 monthly; daily step 0.245 → 0.409 on Aug 10) with no latency cost.
  • Support did not buckle. Median resolution time improved (21.7 h Mar → 16.1 h Jul) despite 40% more ticket volume in July.

Open questions

  1. What drove Helix Genomics' ~1,500× August surge in nova-reasoning document_analysis — a new workload, a contract/pricing change, or a one-off backfill? If it is one-off, ex-Helix August revenue (~$6.17M) is the true run-rate and September may look like July again.
  2. How much of the $702K/mo is recoverable? 24 critical tickets in Jun–Jul carried churn-risk language ("Evaluating alternative providers", "Reducing usage until latency is fixed") — did those specific customers churn in Aug/Sep? (August churned MRR was only $26.1K, suggesting no — worth confirming by name.)
  3. SLA exposure continued past the June churn: July credits were $154.3K across 95 invoices (including credits to Helix, McCarthy and Werner-Carter). Are other Enterprise accounts at risk of the same credit-then-churn pattern?
  4. Process: why did a 2.6× latency regression take 19 days to declare and 63 days to fix? The fix (DEP-0041) added a long-prompt latency canary — has it been backfilled to all regions/components, and are there pre-release latency regression gates for the three components that have rolled back (inference-engine, router, rate-limiter)?
  5. Data inconsistencies to correct: INC-2026-0144's end_time (Aug 29) precedes its fix deployment DEP-0052 (Aug 31); DEP-0021 (cause of the worst incident) is not flagged rolled_back=1 (fixed forward); deployment version numbers are non-monotonic across months (v5.2.7 → v2.8.0 → v2.9.1 for inference-engine), making "v2.8" ambiguous.
  6. Commercial response to the noisy neighbor: Helix's batch ran 12 days before admission control landed (DEP-0052, Aug 31). What was the SLA/commercial response to the other US-East customers degraded during that window (P95 761 → 859 ms)?
